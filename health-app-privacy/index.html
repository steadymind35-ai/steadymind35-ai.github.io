Steady Mind 35 – Personal Health Tracker: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026

This policy covers the Steady Mind 35 Personal Health Tracker ("the app"), a private tool that connects to Google health services. It is separate from our mailing list, which is covered by our main privacy notice.

Who we are. Steady Mind Thirty Five, 50 Argyll Road, London, SE18 6PJ, United Kingdom. Contact: hello@steadymind35.com.

Who can use the app. The app is for the account owner's personal use only. It is not offered to the public, followers, subscribers or customers.

What data the app accesses. With the account owner's explicit permission, given through Google's consent screen, the app may read health and activity data, such as steps, activity, sleep and heart rate, only for the data types shown on that screen.

How the data is used. Only to show the account owner their own habits and trends. The data is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not used to train AI models. It is never published, and Steady Mind 35 content never uses it in a way that identifies anyone.

Sharing. The data is not shared with third parties, except the service providers needed to run the app, and only as Google's API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements, allows.

Storage and security. The data is kept only as long as it is needed for personal tracking, and it is protected with reasonable security measures. You can disconnect the app at any time in your Google Account under third-party connections, and any stored data will be deleted on request.

Lawful basis. Explicit consent (UK GDPR Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)).

Your rights. You can ask to access, correct or delete your data, or withdraw consent, by emailing hello@steadymind35.com. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Not medical advice. The app shows data for personal interest only. It does not diagnose, treat or give medical advice.

Struggling right now? In the UK, call Samaritans free on 116 123 (24/7), or call 999 in an emergency.
